# Vicenç Mut and the 1683 map of Mallorca — research dossier

Background research for the *mapa-mut* outreach website, on the author **Vicenç
Mut i Armengol** (Vicente Mut, 1614–1687) and his 1683 map *Insula Maioricae*.

Compiled from a multi-source, fact-checked sweep (24 sources fetched, ~80
falsifiable claims extracted and adversarially verified; none refuted). Each
section cites the sources it rests on. Where sources disagree or a point is
attribution-dependent, it is flagged.

---

## 1. The author: Vicenç Mut i Armengol (1614–1687)

**Life.** Born **25 October 1614** in Ciutat de Mallorca (Palma) and died **27
April 1687** in the same city, where he was buried in the Seu (Cathedral).
Trained in his youth by the Jesuits, he entered military and civil service rather
than the order. [RAH; UIB *anymut*; Enciclopèdia; Wikipedia ES/EN]

**Offices and roles.** Mut was a polymath who combined several careers:
- **Military engineer** of the Kingdom of Mallorca, holding the rank of **sergent
  major** (sergeant major) of the city of Palma; he directed fortification works
  in the Balearics and took part in repairing Palma's sea walls. Sources credit
  him with roughly **47 years** as engineer of the kingdom's fortifications, in
  service from about **1640**. [RAH; Enciclopèdia; balearsfaciencia.org]
- **Cronista general (Official Chronicler) of the Kingdom of Mallorca**, appointed
  by the jurats on **15 January 1641** and holding the post until his death in
  1687. [UIB *anymut*; RAH; academia.edu]
- Also a **doctor of law**, city **jurat**, and **accountant** of Mallorca.
  [Enciclopèdia; es.wikipedia]

**Historical work.** His best-known book is the ***Historia del Reino de
Mallorca*** (Tomo II, printed 1650 by the heirs of Gabriel Guasp), written as the
**continuation of Joan Dameto's** *Historia General del reyno Baleárico*,
extending the chronicle to 1650 (covering 1311–1650). [RAH; mcnbiografias;
academia.edu; researchgate]

**Astronomy.** Mut is counted among the **most distinguished astronomers of
17th-century Spain** and a pioneer who helped introduce the "new astronomy" into
the Hispanic world. His astronomical works, all printed in Mallorca, include:
- ***De sole alfonsino restituto*** (1649), on the solar diameter and Earth's
  shadow;
- ***Observationes motuum caelestium*** (1666);
- ***Cometarum anni MDCLXV*** (1666), a short physico-mathematical treatise on
  the comet(s) of 1664–1665, built on more than twenty years of observations of
  eclipses, planetary motions and parallaxes.

He corresponded intensely with the Jesuit astronomer **Giovanni Battista
Riccioli** (1598–1671), who folded many of Mut's observations into his own
publications and **named a lunar crater "Mutus"** in his honour. He is sometimes
described as the first Spanish scientist to cite Galileo. [larramendi.es
(Navarro Brotons study); balearsfaciencia.org; omnesmag; mcnbiografias]

**Military architecture.** He published a treatise on fortification, ***Tratado
de arquitectura militar*** / *Arquitectura militar* (Mallorca, **1664**), regarded
as one of the outstanding Spanish works in that field. [mcnbiografias; RAH;
Enciclopèdia]

## 2. The 1683 map — *Insula Maioricae*

**Authorship.** The map is attributed to Mut as **draftsman/author** through the
cartouche inscription **"D[on] Vicetius Mut deli."** (*delineavit* — "drew it"),
placed among the graphic scales. Titled ***Insula Maioricae*** (a variant title
reads *"Illa Maioricae. Latitudo G.39.35 Longitudo 25.25"*). Dated **1683**. It
is often called the **first printed map of Mallorca made by a resident of the
island**. [IGN Cartoteca 023527; Commons; Wikipedia; fabian.balearweb.net]

**Dedication.** The map carries a Latin dedication in a cornucopia cartouche,
offered by the kingdom's jurats to the viceroy **Manuel de Sentmenat i de Lanuza**
("Emmanueli Sentmanat" / "Emanueli Setmanat"). [IGN; fabian.balearweb.net]

**Engravers and collaborators named on the map.** Contributors credited in the
cartouche include **Nicolau Rossinyol Zagranada** (Nicolás Russiñol), **Antoni
Custurer**, **Pere Jeroni Armengol** (Pedro Geo. Armengol), **Joan Ferragut**,
**Antoni Busquets**, and **Damià Caçà**. (The exact division of the engraving
work between these hands is not spelled out by the sources and should be treated
as a collective attribution.) [IGN; fabian.balearweb.net]

**Cartographic characteristics.**
- **Orientation:** unusually, the map is oriented with the **south-east at the
  top** (a "south-oriented" map). North is marked by a fleur-de-lis on the compass
  roses. [es.wikipedia; fabian.balearweb.net; IGN]
- **Scale & size:** approximately **1:123,000**; the IGN copy measures **42 × 58
  cm** on a **49.5 × 65.5 cm** sheet. [IGN 023527; Alamy]
- **Content:** Latin legends with toponymy in **Catalan and Latin**; **two
  eight-wind compass roses** at sea; an ungraduated frame; fortifications;
  episcopal and parish divisions; relief shown by **profiled/felled mountain
  silhouettes**; the coast enhanced with **vertical hatching**. Mut is said to
  have **determined Palma's latitude himself** and to have located the cities,
  towns (viles), castles and colleges. [IGN; es.wikipedia; fabian.balearweb.net]
- **Accuracy:** the perimeter and proportions are more modern than earlier maps,
  with a regularised coastline; of ~70 coastal toponyms, roughly **96% are
  considered correct**. [fabian.balearweb.net]

**The legend — *Notarū Explicatio*.** A notation key distinguishes the place
classes drawn by symbol (not by name): **bishopric (Episcopatus), abbey
(Abbatia), parish town (Villa parochialis), hamlet (Pagus), fortified castle
(Castrum munitum), house of studies / college (Domus studiorum), watchtowers
(Turres speculatoriae) and fire/warning towers (Turres ignibus…)**, plus the two
**civitates**, Mallorca (Palma) and Alcúdia. This legend is the source of the
type vocabulary used in the *mapa-mut* dataset. [IGN; project map reading]

## 3. Editions and holdings

- **Original:** copperplate (calcographic) print of **1683**.
- **1946 reprint:** re-struck in **Madrid at the Calcografía Nacional** by
  **Adolfo Rupérez**, at the expense of the **Diputación Provincial de Baleares**.
  [Alamy; IGN]
- A **reduced engraving/reproduction** was made by **Juan Umbert in 1886**.
  [fabian.balearweb.net]
- **Holdings:** a copy is held by the **Institut Cartogràfic de Catalunya**
  (Barcelona), 42 × 58 cm; the **IGN Cartoteca** holds a copy under signatura
  **32-H-20**. [Alamy; IGN 023527]

## 4. Relation to the later Despuig map (1785)

Mallorcan cartography before the 19th century is often framed by **three landmark
maps about a century apart**: **Joan Binimelis** (c. 1593–1595), **Vicenç Mut**
(1683), and the map sponsored by canon **Antoni Despuig i Dameto** (1784–1785).

The **Despuig map** was engraved by **Josep Muntaner Moner** in **1785** at a
scale of about **1:72,000** (finer than Mut's ~1:123,000), published by Julián
Ballester, its initiative likely arising within the **Sociedad Económica
Mallorquina de Amigos del País** in the Enlightenment context. A reduced 1829
version (IGN 32-H-18) was cut by Lorenzo María Muntaner at ~1:210,000. The Despuig
map is the sibling project of *despuig* (the twin of this repository); Mut's is
the earlier, cruder, south-oriented precursor. [Dialnet 3237802; IGN 023524;
es.wikipedia *Escuela cartográfica mallorquina*]
